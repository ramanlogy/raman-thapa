

## Nepal’s Real Estate Market Hits a Three-Year High: What the Latest Surge Means for Property Buyers
August, 10, 2026 | by Reejan  Kushma | img https://api.legendsinvestment.com.np/blogs/46/challnkhel2 JPG_1786353303.JPG

Nepal’s real estate market has entered an interesting phase. After several years of relatively subdued activity, property transactions accelerated sharply toward the end of Fiscal Year 2025/26. Government revenue from real estate transactions reached Rs. 60.74 billion , the highest level recorded in three years.   But what is behind this sudden increase? The answer lies in a combination of changing tax policies, land-use regulations, declining interest rates and renewed confidence among property buyers and investors. More importantly, the recent surge offers a useful glimpse into where Nepal’s property market could be heading next. More importantly, the recent surge offers a useful glimpse into where Nepal’s property market could be heading next.   A Strong Finish to Fiscal Year 2025/26 According to data from the Department of Land Management and Archives, Nepal collected Rs. 60.74 billion in real estate transaction revenue during FY 2025/26. That is significantly higher than the Rs. 42.42 billion collected in FY 2023/24 and Rs. 41.16 billion in FY 2022/23.   The most dramatic increase came during the final month of the fiscal year. Between mid-June and mid-July, real estate transaction revenue reached approximately Rs. 11.95 billion , the highest monthly collection of the fiscal year. During the same period, 70,480 property purchase and sale deeds were registered, compared with 54,724 in the previous month. The numbers clearly show that buyers and sellers were moving quickly. But this was not simply a case of people suddenly deciding to buy property.   Why Did Property Transactions Surge?   1. Changes in Capital Gains Tax One of the biggest factors behind the rush was the government's decision to increase the capital gains tax on property sales effective at the beginning of the new fiscal year.   Under the revised structure reported by the government and media, the tax on gains from properties held for more than five years increased from 5% to 7.5% , while properties held for less than five years moved from 7.5% to 10% . The revised rates took effect from July 17, 2026. That created a clear incentive for people who were already planning to buy or sell property to complete their transactions before the new rates came into effect. The result was a significant last-minute rush.   On July 16 alone, 10,968 land transactions were recorded nationwide—the highest number ever recorded in a single day according to the Department of Land Management and Archives. The government collected approximately Rs. 1.737 billion from those transactions that day. This demonstrates just how strongly policy changes can influence property-market activity.   2. Renewed Restrictions on Land Subdivision Tax was not the only factor. Land subdivision rules also played an important role. Nepal has been working through land classification requirements under its land-use regulations. As of late June, only 340 of the country's 753 local governments had completed land classification, leaving many municipalities facing restrictions on land subdivision.   For buyers and sellers involved in land transactions, uncertainty around subdivision created another reason to complete deals before restrictions returned. Land subdivision activity increased sharply during the final month of FY 2025/26, while subdivision cases subsequently dropped after restrictions were restored in municipalities that had not completed classification.   For the property market, this highlights an increasingly important reality: Regulatory certainty matters almost as much as price.   3. Lower Interest Rates Are Supporting the Recovery There is another factor worth watching: borrowing costs. According to industry representatives cited by the Kathmandu Post, declining bank interest rates have contributed to a gradual decline in real estate activity, with some investors becoming more willing to move money from bank deposits toward land and housing.   This is significant because interest rates influence the affordability of property purchases, particularly for buyers who rely on financing. When borrowing becomes more affordable, property that previously appeared financially difficult can become more accessible. That can gradually translate into increased demand.   Locations Matter More than Market Noise Tax changes can affect transaction costs. Interest rates can change. Regulations can evolve. But the fundamental value of a property continues to depend heavily on its location, accessibility, infrastructure and future development potential. A strategically located property with good road access, reliable utilities, planned infrastructure and proximity to growing urban centres can remain valuable beyond short-term market fluctuations.   Infrastructure Is Becoming a Bigger Differentiator As Nepal's property market becomes more organized, buyers are increasingly looking beyond the size and price of a plot. They are asking: Is the road properly planned? Is water supply available? Is electricity accessible? Is drainage properly managed? Are the land documents clear? Is the surrounding area developing? Can the property support long-term residential use?   These questions matter because buying land is not simply about purchasing a piece of ground. It is about buying into the future of a location.   The Rise of Planned Land Development The recent market activity also highlights an important shift in Nepal's real estate sector: the growing importance of planned land development . Instead of purchasing isolated plots without adequate infrastructure, buyers are increasingly interested in developments where roads, drainage, utilities, open spaces, and community planning are considered from the beginning. This creates a more structured environment for both homeowners and investors. For developers, it also raises the standard. Simply dividing land into plots is no longer enough. Long-term value increasingly depends on how well an entire development is planned and executed.   A Changing Real Estate Landscape Nepal's real estate market is moving through a period of transition. The latest figures show that demand has not disappeared. In fact, when policy certainty, financing conditions and buyer confidence align, transaction activity can increase rapidly.  At the same time, regulatory changes are making the market more complex. The government's renewed focus on land classification, subdivision and taxation means that buyers and developers must pay greater attention to compliance and documentation. There is also a broader move toward formalization. The Department of Land Management and Archives has announced that real estate transactions worth more than Rs. 30 million in metropolitan and sub-metropolitan areas must be conducted through licensed real estate companies. This could gradually encourage greater transparency and professionalism within the sector.   What comes Next for Nepal's Property Market? The next few months will be important. The extraordinary transaction volume seen at the end of FY 2025/26 was partly caused by buyers attempting to get ahead of tax and regulatory changes. Activity may therefore cool from those exceptional levels. But the underlying signals are worth watching. Real estate revenue has recovered strongly compared with the previous two fiscal years. Interest rates have become more supportive. Buyers remain interested in land and housing. And demand for well-located, properly planned property continues to exist. The market may not be returning to the speculative boom periods of the past. Instead, Nepal could be moving toward a more selective real estate market—one where location, infrastructure, documentation, and long-term usability matter increasingly more than speculation alone.   The Bigger Picture The three-year high in real estate transaction revenue is more than just a number. It shows that Nepal's property market remains highly responsive to government policy, financing conditions, and buyer confidence. The recent rush also provides a lesson for property buyers: Don't buy simply because everyone else is buying. Buy when the property itself makes sense. A good property should offer more than short-term price potential. It should provide accessibility, infrastructure, legal clarity, livability and long-term value. As Nepal's cities continue to expand and surrounding areas become increasingly connected, the opportunities in real estate will likely become more location-specific and quality-driven. For today's buyer, the question is therefore not simply: "Will property prices go up?" The better question is: "Am I buying the right property in the right location for the long term?" That is the question that will continue to shape Nepal's next phase of real estate growth.

## Anti - Money Laundering | Nepal Real Estate
May, 21, 2026 | by Reejan  Kushma | img https://api.legendsinvestment.com.np/blogs/44/Screenshot 2026 05 21 170509 png_1779364574.png

What is Anti-Money Laundering? Money laundering is the process of making illegally obtained money appear legitimate. AML — Anti-Money Laundering — is the set of laws, regulations, and practices designed to detect and prevent this. The process typically happens in three stages: Placement (injecting dirty cash into the financial system), Layering (obscuring its trail through complex transactions), and Integration (the money re-enters the economy looking clean). Real estate is uniquely useful at all three stages.   Legal Framework in Nepal Nepal's primary AML law is the Asset (Money) Laundering Prevention Act, 2008 (amended 2014) . The Financial Information Unit (FIU-Nepal) under Nepal Rastra Bank is the apex authority overseeing compliance and intelligence. The Department of Money Laundering Investigation (DMLI) under the Attorney General's Office handles prosecution.   Why Real Estate Is a Prime Target? Globally, real estate is among the top three sectors exploited for money laundering. In Nepal, the risk is amplified by rapid urbanization, informal cash norms, and limited digital transaction trails. Property is attractive to criminals for several reasons: prices are subjective and negotiable, large sums can be moved in a single deal, ownership can be concealed through companies or nominees, and the asset appreciates, turning dirty money into legitimate wealth.   "In Nepal's fast-growing cities — Kathmandu, Pokhara, Bharatpur — land prices have risen dramatically, making property an ideal vehicle to park and legitimize illicit funds."   Factors specific to Nepal that raise the risk include the widespread use of hundi (informal remittance), heavy reliance on cash in property deals, limited beneficial ownership transparency, and a large informal economy. Nepal has also faced scrutiny from FATF-linked bodies regarding AML gaps in designated sectors, including real estate.   How Criminals Do It ✓ Over/under-invoicing vs. market valuation ✓ Cash structuring (smurfing) below thresholds ✓ Shell company purchases to hide ownership ✓ Third-party payments by unrelated persons ✓ Hundi-funded purchases (foreign dirty money) ✓ Rapid property flipping to create fake "profit."   🔴Red Flags to look for Large cash payment with no documented source of funds Buyer is uninterested in the property condition or location Price significantly above or below the market valuation PEP (politician/official) buying with unexplained wealth Refund requested to a different account after cancellation Payment made by an unrelated third party Newly registered company buying high-value land Reluctance or evasion when asked for standard documents Funds from high-risk jurisdictions or via hundi   ✅ Due diligence checklist ✓ Verify citizenship certificate or passport ✓ Collect PAN, cross-check with IRD ✓ Screen against PEP and sanctions lists ✓ For companies: get registration + beneficial owners list ✓ Request 6–12 months of bank statements ✓ Verify income is consistent with purchase price ✓ Confirm foreign remittance via a formal bank (not hundi) ✓ Compare price with malpot valuation + market rates ✓ Check Lalpurja history for rapid previous transfers ✓ Ensure all payments via licensed bank — no undocumented cash ✓ Retain all records for a minimum of 5 years (legal requirement) If you suspect it — 5 steps 1. Do NOT tip off the suspect — alerting them is a criminal offence 2. Document all red flags, dates, communications, and copies of ID 3. Escalate to your compliance officer / MLRO if applicable 4. File an STR with FIU-Nepal within 3 working days using the prescribed format — Nepal Rastra Bank, Baluwatar, KTM 5. Cooperate with DMLI if contacted · You are legally protected for good-faith reporting   📍 FIU-Nepal — NRB, Baluwatar 📍 DMLI — Attorney General's Office 📍 Local Malpot Karyalay   Penalties under Nepal's AML Act Committing money laundering: Up to 10 yrs prison + fine Failing to file an STR: Fine + imprisonment Tipping off a suspect: Criminal liability Not maintaining KYC records: Regulatory penalty Facilitating laundering: Criminal complicity Property used in laundering: State seizure/forfeiture   The Cost of Looking the Other Way Money laundering through real estate does not just harm abstract financial systems — it drives up property prices for ordinary Nepalis, enables corruption, and funds criminal enterprises. Every professional who turns a blind eye becomes part of the problem. The good news: Nepal's legal framework exists and is being strengthened. FIU-Nepal is increasingly active. International pressure through FATF processes is encouraging tighter enforcement. And citizens, professionals, and institutions who apply the principles in this guide make a genuine difference.   Compliance is not just Legal — it's a Civic Responsibility. If you are a real estate professional, ensure your KYC processes are in place. If you are a buyer or seller, transact through formal channels. If something feels wrong, report it. Nepal's AML infrastructure exists to support you.

## The Complete Guide to Buying Land in Nepal: Legal Steps, Costs & Process

Investing in land in Nepal is not just a financial decision—it’s a legacy move. Whether you're a first-time buyer, a returning Nepali from abroad, or a seasoned investor, understanding the legal and procedural steps is crucial to ensure a smooth and secure transaction. This comprehensive guide outlines the essential steps, legal requirements, and costs associated with purchasing land in Nepal.​   Step-by-Step Land Purchase Process in Nepal   1. Identify and Select the Property Begin by researching and selecting the desired land. Utilize real estate agents, online listings, or personal networks to find properties that meet your criteria. Ensure the land is free from disputes and has clear ownership.​   2. Conduct Due Diligence Before proceeding, perform thorough checks: Verify Ownership : Obtain the original land ownership certificate (Lalpurja) and confirm the seller's identity through their citizenship certificate. Check for Encumbrances : Ensure the land is free from mortgages, liens, or legal disputes by obtaining an Encumbrance Certificate from the local Land Revenue Office. Land Use and Zoning : Confirm the land's zoning status (residential, agricultural, commercial) and any restrictions with the local municipal office. Tax Clearance : Request the latest tax payment receipt to ensure all dues are cleared.   3. Negotiate and Draft the Sale Agreement Once satisfied, negotiate the terms and draft a sale agreement (Rajinama) detailing: Purchase price and payment terms Property details, including boundaries and size Responsibilities of both buyer and seller​ This agreement should be signed in the presence of witnesses and notarized to ensure its legality.   4. Obtain Necessary Approvals and Documents Prepare the following documents for registration: Original Lalpurja Signed Sale Agreement Citizenship Certificates of both the buyer and the seller Tax Clearance Certificate Land Survey and Zoning Approval​   5. Register the Land at the Land Revenue Office Both parties must visit the local Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya) to officially register the transaction. The office will verify all documents, and upon approval, the buyer will receive a new Lalpurja in their name.   6. Pay Applicable Taxes and Fees The buyer is responsible for paying: Registration Fee : Typically 4-6% of the property's value. Stamp Duty : 1-2% of the property's value. Capital Gains Tax : Applicable if reselling the land; 2.5% for individuals and 10% for companies. Local Development Fee : 1.5% of the property's value in most areas.   7. Take Possession and Update Records After registration and payment: Take physical possession of the land. Install boundary markers or fencing to prevent encroachment. Inform local authorities and utility providers of the ownership change. Regularly check government records to prevent fraudulent claims. ​   Special Considerations for Non-Resident Nepalis (NRNs)   NRNs can purchase land in Nepal under specific conditions: Ownership Restrictions : NRNs must not possess any existing land registered in their or their family's name. Application Process : Submit an application to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) with necessary documents, including the NRN identification card and foreign citizenship certificate. Land Purchase Limits : For instance, in the Kathmandu Valley, NRNs can buy up to two Ropanis of land. Repatriation : While NRNs can sell land in Nepal, repatriating the proceeds abroad is restricted.   Essential Documents Checklist   Original Land Ownership Certificate (Lalpurja) Signed Sale Agreement (Rajinama) Citizenship Certificates of Buyer and Seller Tax Clearance Certificate Land Survey and Zoning Approval Encumbrance Certificate Notarized Documents   Timeline and Costs   Duration : A straightforward land transfer can be completed within 2 to 4 weeks. However, more complex cases may take longer, potentially extending to 2-3 months. Total Costs : Including registration fees, taxes, and legal fees, the total cost can range from 6-10% of the property's value. ​   Final Tips Always engage a legal professional to assist with the process. Ensure all documents are verified and notarized. Be proactive in following up with authorities to prevent delays. Maintain copies of all documents and receipts for future reference.​   By adhering to these steps and ensuring all legal requirements are met, you can confidently invest in land in Nepal, securing both your financial future and a piece of the country's rich heritage.

A cap rate is a metric used to evaluate the potential return on a real estate investment. It’s calculated by dividing the property’s net operating income (NOI) by its current market value. Essentially, it tells you the rate of return you can expect on your investment. For example, if you purchase a property for NPR 5 Crore that generates NPR 50 lakhs in net operating income annually, the cap rate would be 10%.   Why Cap Rates Matter? Cap rates are a valuable tool for comparing different investment opportunities. They help investors understand the profitability of a property relative to its price. A higher cap rate typically indicates a higher return on investment, but it can also signify higher risk.
